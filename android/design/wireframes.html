  • The position row leads with the contract, not the engine (PR #3). Cost-to-close earns the row twice over: it is the gain/loss that matters on short premium, and it is the exact number the 2× stop watches. Stock price moved to detail.
  • Attribution became a first-class visual (§6.1, and §2's distrust rule). Delta-driven and vega-driven reds are drawn unalike everywhere they appear: row, detail, card, widget, notification.

Adopted for the contract schema pass — options[]

  • Settled in PR #3 review: the card carries options[] of {id, label, kind, is_default, requires_reason} and POST /v1/decisions/{id} takes {option_id, reason?}. kind is one of accept | reject | defer | branch, added so §2's veto-rate metric keeps a stable category to count while the labels stay the engine's own words.

    The payoff is bigger than the wording. All three cards here now render from the same function: it picks buttons or a radio list by looking at whether any option has kind: "branch". The card component stopped needing to know the type list — which was the actual problem, since that list has grown twice already.

    And kind earns itself immediately. On the vega card, "Hold" is kind: reject — it counts toward veto rate although the word "veto" never appears on screen. On the branch card no option is a reject, so it contributes nothing to that metric, which is correct: declining to re-enter a name is not a vote against the rubric. Under the old fixed verbs both cards would have POSTed approve and the metric would have quietly counted the wrong things.

    One thing is_default must not become. It is sent only where the engine genuinely has a proposal — the entry card, which screened the trade and cleared the APR gate. The vega card sends none, because it exists precisely where the 2× rule and the attribution disagree; Exit now and Hold therefore render with equal weight. That matters beyond tidiness: RUBRIC names strict-stops vs. vega-hold as a backtest A/B, and a card that nudges toward exiting biases the forward record of which arm was chosen. Snooze stays visually lighter everywhere, since deferring isn't a third position on the question.

    On the branch card is_default is drawn as an "engine's pick" label with nothing pre-selected and Confirm disabled until the operator taps. Pre-selecting it would make agreeing cost one tap and disagreeing cost two — plus a written reason where requires_reason applies — and §2 judges the strategy on whether veto rate falls across rubric versions. A default that nudges makes that number fall for reasons that have nothing to do with the rubric. Same failure mode as the veto chips: an affordance quietly manufacturing the data that grades the system. The journal should also record whether the submitted option was the default, or the audit trail can't tell a considered choice from a rubber stamp.

    Superseded: the italic mapping line under each card used to translate domain verbs back to approve|veto|snooze. It now just prints the option ids and kinds — no translation table for the app to keep in sync.

The other thing the row change needs from the contract

  • /v1/positions doesn't name the fields the new row is built on. The sketch reads "per-underlying wheel state, open legs, P&L bands, margin, next_auto_exit" — enough for the old row, not for this one. It needs, per leg: contract identity (right, strike, expiry, qty), the credit received at open, and the current cost to close. Stock legs need qty and cost basis.

    One band subtlety worth writing into the rule. Credit received is a settled cash fact — it has no floor/policy/mid. The cost to close is a live quote and is fully banded. So on a short-premium row exactly one of the two numbers carries a band, and the unrealized P&L inherits its band from the close side alone. That reads as an exception to §6.7 but isn't one; it's the band rule applied correctly to a number that has already happened.

Superseded — the old verb problem

  • The action verbs don't survive the new card types. approve|veto|snooze reads correctly on an entry proposal. On a vega hold_or_exit card, "approve" means sell and "veto" means hold — a coin-flip to read at 6am, and the gate matrix's own phrasing ("at least get approval before selling") only makes sense if you already know which way round it goes. The post-loss branch card is worse: it's three-way, so it has no veto at all, and has to POST an approve that secretly carries which branch.

    Drawn here the pragmatic way — UI shows the domain verb, POST keeps the contract verb, each card carrying a small italic mapping line. It works, but it means the app maintains a translation table that has to stay in sync with the engine, and every new card type adds a row to it.

    Cleaner alternative, if the schema pass is open to it: give the card an options[] array of {id, label, is_default, requires_reason} and let POST /v1/decisions/{id} take {option_id, reason?}. The engine then names its own verbs, the app renders whatever arrives, and requires_reason generalises the veto-reason rule instead of hardcoding it. Given the type list has now grown twice, betting on a third seems safer than betting against.
